Our story · Since 2011

Two people, one borrowed acre, and a lot of rocks.

Bramblecut started as a quarter-mile of rented hillside and a truck bed of lettuce. Fifteen seasons on, it is 22 acres, five growers and 310 kitchens, and we still pull rocks every spring.

How it started

From a truck bed to a hollow of our own

Mae Lindqvist and Tomás Rivera met pulling weeds on someone else's farm. In 2011 a retired dairyman named Walt let them work one acre of his worst ground for the price of a weekly bag of vegetables. The soil was thin, the hill was steep, and the rocks came up faster than the carrots.

They sold that first summer from the tailgate at the Viroqua market. Two years later, 24 neighbors paid up front for a season of crates, and the CSA was born out of a borrowed walk-in cooler and a very long spreadsheet.

In 2017 the hollow down the road came up for sale: 22 acres of south-facing slope, a spring-fed creek, and a barn with half a roof. We bought it with a farm loan and a lot of member prepayments, and every crate since has come out of that ground.

We plant for the people who paid in February. That is the whole business plan, and it has held for fifteen years.

Mae Lindqvist, co-owner and grower

Growing instructions

How we farm, on the back of the packet

Four rules we wrote on the barn door the week we bought the hollow. They still decide most things.

Feed the soil, not the plant

Cover crops on every bed over winter, compost from our own piles, and rotations planned four years ahead.

Nothing sprayed we would not eat

Certified organic since 2014. When a bug wins a crop, we lose it and write about it in the note.

Share the risk and the good years

Members pay up front and carry the weather with us. In a bumper year, the crates get heavier.

Nobody leaves without dinner

Sliding-scale shares, SNAP at the stand, and work shares mean the price never decides who eats.
